Blockchain Student Association hackathon, Belem Capital challenge

fulcrum

My project for the Belem Capital DeFi risk dependency challenge. You give it a DeFi position, it digs down to what that position actually depends on (markets, collateral, custodians, oracles), estimates what you'd lose if one of those breaks, and tells you if it's worth pulling out.

The problem

Belem's example: a book split into 10 pockets, one gets hit. Stay or leave?

Leaving costs something you know (slippage, lost yield). Staying costs something you don't. The brief asks for a tool that puts numbers on both sides using real on-chain data, so the decision isn't just gut feeling at 3am.

1. Find what it depends on

Markets, borrowers, oracles and token issuers, pulled automatically.

2. Estimate the loss

Borrower by borrower, then the vault's share, then yours.

3. Stay or leave

Compare the average loss with what it costs to leave.

Step 1: getting the data

Three data sources, all saved.

Morpho's API

For each vault: which markets it lends to, how much, and every single borrower in those markets, paged until the borrower shares add up to the market total.

Ethereum itself

Every oracle contract is called directly to see which price feeds it uses, and ask each feed for its name. All at the same block, cross-checked against Morpho's API.

Price history

Daily BTC and ETH prices from Bitstamp, to count how often big drops actually happened.

Everything gets saved with a SHA-256 hash, so the demo runs offline and you can check nothing changed. One script grabs fresh data.

Step 2: dependencies

One deposit in Steakhouse USDC, layer by layer.

The issuer/custodian part is a small list I wrote by hand, with a source for each token. The rest comes straight from the chain.

Step 2: the main finding

Some oracles can't see the thing that would hurt lenders.

A lending market only liquidates people when its oracle says the collateral dropped. So I checked what every oracle actually reads. If cbBTC depegs but the oracle only reads BTC/USD, the market thinks everything is fine: nobody gets liquidated, and people can even deposit the cheap token and borrow against it.

Can see it

There's a feed for the token itself, like WBTC / BTC.

Only the issuer rate

Like weETH/ETH exchange rate. That's what the protocol says it's worth, not what it trades at.

Can't see it

Nothing about the token at all, like cbBTC priced with BTC / USD only.

Step 3: estimating the loss

Borrower by borrower.

loss on a borrower = max(0, debt - collateral x (1 - drop) x (1 - selling discount))
liquidators step in only if bonus x (1 - selling discount) >= 1, bonus = 1 / (0.3 x LLTV + 0.7)
oracle can't see a depeg: people borrow the market's spare cash against the cheap token

Two cases. "All at once": the price gaps down before anyone can liquidate. "Slowly": liquidations happen in time, but only if the oracle sees the drop and liquidators still make money selling the collateral. One borrower's extra collateral can't cover someone else's hole. Then the vault takes its share of each market's loss, and you take your share of the vault.

Step 4: the decision

Average loss vs. cost of leaving.

cost of leaving = interest you stop earning while you're out + gas
chance = how often a drop this big happened before (or your own guess)
leaving is worth it if chance > cost of leaving / loss you'd avoid
